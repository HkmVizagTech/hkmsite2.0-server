On 14 November 1977, in the holy town of Vrindavan, His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Srila Prabhupada left this world while his disciples chanted the Holy Names of Krishna around him. Every year, on the corresponding lunar date during the month of Kartik, devotees in ISKCON temples around the world observe his disappearance day. ISKCON Vaishnava calendars list the observance for 13 November 2026.

It is a day of mixed feelings. There is sorrow at the loss of his physical presence, and there is deep gratitude for everything he gave. Above all, it is a day to ask a question that every follower of Srila Prabhupada must answer for themselves: how do I stay connected with him now?

Why a "Disappearance" Day?

In the Vaishnava tradition, the passing of a pure devotee is not called a death. It is called a disappearance, because such a soul simply leaves our sight and returns to the Lord's service in the spiritual world. Lord Krishna explains in the Bhagavad-gita (Bg. 4.9) that one who understands the transcendental nature of His appearance and activities does not take birth again after leaving the body, but comes to Him. A pure devotee who has dedicated his entire life to Krishna goes to Krishna.

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura expressed this in a short English poem that Srila Prabhupada himself quoted on more than one occasion. It begins: "He reasons ill who tells that Vaishnavas die." The poem goes on to explain that a Vaishnava lives on in sound, through his words and teachings, and that his followers continue to feel his presence through them.

Vani and Vapu

The Vaishnava acharyas speak of two ways of associating with the spiritual master. Vapu refers to his physical presence. Vani refers to his words and instructions. Srila Prabhupada taught that while physical association is a great blessing, association through the guru's instructions is more important and more lasting, because it never ends. A disciple who follows the instructions stays in the guru's association even if they have never met in person.

This understanding is a source of tremendous hope. Most people who come to Krishna consciousness today were born after 1977. They never saw Srila Prabhupada with their own eyes. And yet, through his books, his recorded lectures and the example of his followers, they meet him every day.

The Legacy He Left

His books

Srila Prabhupada considered his books his most important contribution. He translated and commented on the Bhagavad-gita, the Srimad-Bhagavatam, the Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita, The Nectar of Devotion, Sri Ishopanishad and many other works, often working late into the night. Even in his final months in Vrindavan, when his body was very weak, he continued to translate the Srimad-Bhagavatam. For his followers, reading these books is the most direct way of hearing from him.

His movement

In 1965, at the age of sixty-nine, Srila Prabhupada travelled by cargo ship from Calcutta to America, carrying a few trunks of books and very little money, to fulfil the order of his spiritual master, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, to preach Krishna consciousness in English. In 1966 he founded the International Society for Krishna Consciousness in New York. Within a little more than a decade, temples, farm communities, schools and restaurants serving prasadam had opened across the world.

His simple programme

Srila Prabhupada gave his followers a practical way of life that anyone can take up: chant the Hare Krishna maha-mantra daily, follow four regulative principles (no meat, fish or eggs; no intoxication; no gambling; no illicit sex), read his books, honour prasadam, and share Krishna consciousness with others. These are the instructions through which he remains present.

How Devotees Observe the Day

In ISKCON temples the disappearance day is typically marked with readings, remembrances and kirtan. Devotees share how they came to Krishna consciousness through Srila Prabhupada's books or followers, read from accounts of his final days, and offer arati and flowers to his murti. Devotees usually fast until noon and then honour a feast offered to Srila Prabhupada.

At home, you can observe the day in simple ways:

  • Fast until noon, if your health permits, and use the morning for chanting and reading.
  • Read from his books, especially the introductions and prefaces, where his own voice comes through very clearly.
  • Listen to a recording of one of his lectures or kirtans.
  • Write a letter of gratitude to him, sharing how his teachings have changed your life.
  • Make a resolution to follow one of his instructions more seriously in the coming year.

Turning Remembrance Into Service

The best way to honour a teacher is to carry forward his work. Srila Prabhupada's mission was to give everyone the opportunity to become Krishna conscious. That mission continues wherever his followers chant in public, distribute his books, serve prasadam and build temples where people can come and learn about Krishna.

In the Bhagavad-gita (Bg. 18.68–69), Krishna says that one who explains this supreme secret to His devotees is guaranteed pure devotional service, and that there is no servant in this world more dear to Him. Srila Prabhupada lived that verse. On his disappearance day, we can each ask how we can live it too, even in a small way.
